A reloadable card, with no account to sign up for

No email, no password, no personal details, no KYC. Your account is a single secret string that only you hold. Top it up in crypto, mint a card against the balance, and load that same card again whenever you like.

Apple Pay Google Pay
  1. Create your account. One click. You are handed a recovery token and a session key — save both, they are the only way back in.
  2. Top up in crypto. Bitcoin, Monero, Litecoin, Ethereum, USDC or USDT. Your balance appears in USD.
  3. Mint a card. Pick Aurora, Shadow or Eclipse. The only requirement is having the balance to cover it.
  4. Load it and spend. Move balance onto the card, reveal the number, and add it to Apple Pay or Google Pay where the card supports it.

Read this before you start. Because there is no email and no identity attached to the account, there is no password reset and no support recovery. If you lose both your token and your session, the balance and every card on the account are gone permanently. Save them somewhere you trust the moment they are shown.

Closed merchant categories57 codes

The issuer closes these merchant types at the network level: every cut, any balance. A charge there fails no matter what the card holds, and repeat attempts get the card itself flagged — so the card shields itself: a warning first, then a lock.

Blocking runs on the merchant's registered category code (MCC): the label on the door decides, not what's in the basket. The first charge at any of these declines and draws a warning; another within a month locks the card. Unlock is self-serve, once a month, and a closed-category charge after an unlock retires the card for good — balance back to your wallet, card fee not refunded.
